When reviewing monthly sales exports, expense sheets, or campaign conversion tables, analysts spend significant effort reconciling column definitions, spotting outliers, and writing executive summaries. This case shows a reliable two-step workflow in SkyBot.

## Goal & Prerequisites [#goal-and-prerequisites]

- **Expected Deliverable**: A structured Markdown review report containing metric summaries, anomaly explanations (>15% variance), formula notes, and a list of items needing human audit.
- **Prerequisites**:
  1. Prepare sanitized CSV or tabular text exports (stripping unrelated PII or banking numbers).
  2. Optionally publish "KPI & Metric Calculation Definitions" in your workspace Knowledge Base.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough [#step-by-step-walkthrough]

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### 1. Upload Data & Align on Schema First

    Upload the CSV inside a SkyBot thread or Project and start with a lightweight schema check: "Inspect the headers and sample rows of the attached CSV. Summarize column meanings, missing values, and any metric definition ambiguities before calculating totals."
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### 2. Confirm Rules & Run the Full Analysis

    Once column interpretations are confirmed, submit the analysis instruction:

    ```text
    Goal: Summarize monthly conversion and average order value by region and product line, flagging variances above 15%.
    Materials: Use only exact figures from the attached CSV and the published "KPI & Metric Calculation Definitions"; never estimate missing entries.
    Deliverables: Output a structured report with explicit formulas and a "Flagged Items for Human Audit" section.
    Boundaries: Produce an internal draft only; never write back to production BI or finance databases.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### 3. Spot-Check Key Totals Before Distribution

    Verify key totals and anomaly rows directly against the raw sheet before incorporating the report into management reviews.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Acceptance Checklist [#acceptance-checklist]

- **Zero Fabricated Estimates**: Missing values must be explicitly reported as missing—never interpolated silently.
- **Schema Before Math**: Confirming column semantics in turn 1 prevents costly miscalculations in turn 2.
